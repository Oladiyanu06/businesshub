# Go-to-market plan — December 2026

Written 19 November 2026. This is a working plan, not a promise. It separates
what can realistically produce cash in two weeks from what cannot.

## 1. Honest position

**SEO will not produce customers by 1 December.** New domains take roughly
4–12 weeks to be indexed and ranked meaningfully, and 3–6 months to produce
steady leads. It is the right long-term channel and it is now set up correctly,
but it is not a December revenue channel.

**The product is Phase 1.** It manages businesses, branches, employees, roles,
permissions, devices and audit. It does **not** yet have sales, inventory,
customers or expenses. The current landing page uses mock-ups that describe
those future modules. Selling on those claims would create refunds and
reputational damage.

Two possible paths:

| Path | What it means | Revenue by early Dec? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Revenue-first pilot** (recommended) | Sell what exists, honestly, to 2–3 multi-branch businesses: staff accountability, roles, branch oversight, audit trail, plus paid onboarding. Promise stock/sales on a dated roadmap. | Yes, small (₦150k–₦450k) |
| **B. Product-first** | Spend 2–4 weeks building sales + inventory + expenses properly, then sell in January. | No |

Recommendation: **run A now, start B immediately after**, and never let sales
copy describe functionality that is not in the product.

## 2. Who to sell to (ideal customer)

- Retail shop, pharmacy, supermarket or distributor with **2–5 branches** and
  **5–40 staff**.
- Currently tracks staff with WhatsApp, a notebook or Excel; no real audit trail.
- Owner-operator is reachable directly and can approve payment.
- Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt; physically close enough for a walk-in demo.
- Pain the product genuinely solves today: *who changed what, who has access,
  which staff belong to which branch, what happens when someone leaves.*

Avoid: single-shop businesses (no branch problem), enterprises (procurement
cycle too slow), anyone who mainly needs POS/inventory today.

## 3. Offer and pricing (starting point — test it)

| Item | Price (NGN) |
| --- | --- |
| Pilot onboarding (setup, employee import, roles, training, 2 branches) | ₦75,000 one-time; first 3 clients ₦50,000 |
| Monthly licence, up to 3 branches / 25 staff | ₦15,000 |
| Monthly licence, larger | ₦25,000 |
| Annual prepay | 10 months for 12 |
| Extra branch | ₦2,500/month |

Rules:

- 50% of onboarding up front, balance on go-live.
- No free-forever tier. A 14-day paid pilot with money-back guarantee is safer
  than free users who never convert.
- Licensing is recorded in the platform's licence fields; invoices are issued
  outside the app (bank transfer, Paystack or Flutterwave payment link).
- Do not sell below the cost of support. One hour of hand-holding per week per
  client is realistic in month one.

## 4. The 14-day plan

Targets for the fortnight: **100 contacts → ~20 conversations → 8 demos → 3 paid
pilots**. That is ₦150k–₦300k cash, not profit — it validates the offer.

### Day 1 — foundations (today)

- [ ] Buy a domain (see §7) and point it at Vercel.
- [ ] Create `hello@yourdomain` (Zoho free or Google Workspace).
- [ ] Set up WhatsApp Business with a catalogue entry and quick replies.
- [ ] Register the site in Google Search Console; submit `sitemap.xml`.
- [ ] Write the one-page PDF offer (template in §6).

### Day 2 — build the list

- 40 named prospects from your own network, church/market associations,
  LinkedIn, and businesses you physically pass.
- Record: business name, owner, phone, branches, staff estimate, source.
- Ask 5 people for warm introductions.

### Days 3–5 — first wave

- 10 WhatsApp messages per day (script in §6), personalised with the business
  name and branch count.
- One LinkedIn post per day (owner-facing, not developer-facing).
- 5 physical walk-ins in one commercial cluster; ask for the owner, leave the
  one-pager.
- Follow up every non-reply after 48 hours, once.

### Days 6–9 — demos

- Run 30-minute demos on the live site with a temporary demo tenant.
- Structure: their current process → the accountability gap → three screens
  (employees, roles, audit) → pilot offer → close on a date.
- Send the proposal within 2 hours of each demo.

### Days 10–14 — close

- Follow up every proposal within 24 hours.
- Ask for the 50% onboarding payment to book the setup slot.
- Onboard the first client within 3 days of payment; import their staff and
  branches, train the administrator, hand over credentials.
- Ask every pilot for one referral, whether they buy or not.

## 5. Channels, ranked by speed to cash

1. **Warm network and referrals** — fastest. One happy owner introduces two more.
2. **Physical walk-ins** in dense commercial areas — high conversion for local B2B.
3. **WhatsApp** — how Nigerian SMEs actually buy. Personal, short, voice notes work.
4. **LinkedIn** — credibility and inbound for slightly larger businesses; slow.
5. **Market/trade associations** — a single association talk can reach 30 owners.
6. **Google Business Profile + local SEO** — free, helps walk-in trust immediately.
7. **Paid ads** — only after a landing page with a demo CTA and one case study exist.
   If testing: Google Search exact-match, ₦5,000/day for 7 days, measure demo requests.
   Meta interest-targeted ads usually burn money for B2B at this stage.

## 6. Scripts and assets

### WhatsApp first message

> Good afternoon [Name]. I'm [Your name] from BUSINESSHUB. We help businesses
> with more than one branch keep staff, roles and access under control — you can
> see who has access to what, and every change is recorded.
>
> I'm onboarding three pilot businesses this month in [area]. Would a 15-minute
> look at your setup be useful? No obligation.

### Follow-up (48 hours later)

> [Name], just checking you saw my message. Happy to send a one-page summary
> instead if a call isn't convenient.

### Walk-in opener

> Good afternoon. I'm looking for the owner or manager. We work with businesses
> that have more than one branch — I'd like to leave a one-page summary and ask
> two questions about how you currently track staff across branches.

### Demo agenda (30 minutes)

1. Their current process (5 min) — listen, do not pitch.
2. The gap: access, accountability, staff movement between branches (5 min).
3. Three screens: employee list, roles and permissions, audit trail (10 min).
4. Pilot scope, price, timeline (5 min).
5. Close: "Shall we start with [branch] on Monday?" (5 min).

### Objections

- **"We already use Excel."** Excel cannot tell you who changed a salary or who
  still has access after leaving. That is the problem we solve.
- **"Too expensive."** One avoided staff dispute or leakage usually exceeds the
  annual licence. Offer the small pilot scope instead of a discount.
- **"No POS/inventory?"** Correct — that is Phase 2, scheduled. The pilot price
  reflects the current scope, and pilot clients get first access.
- **"Can I try it?"** Yes: 14-day paid pilot, money back if it does not fit.

## 7. Brand, domain and trust

- **Buy a domain before any promotion.** `businesshub-pi.vercel.app` signals a
  prototype. A `.com` or `.ng` costs little and is the single biggest trust gain.
- "BusinessHub" is a common name. Check availability and consider a distinct
  brand (`getbusinesshub.com`, `businesshub.ng`, `hubforbusiness.com`).
- Use a business email on that domain, not a personal Gmail.
- Publish privacy and terms pages; say where data is stored and how it is exported.
- Ask NESS ENTERPRISE for permission to publish a short case study with numbers.

## 8. Measuring

Weekly scorecard:

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| New contacts | 50 |
| Replies | 10 |
| Demos booked | 4 |
| Proposals sent | 3 |
| Pilots paid | 1–2 |
| Cash collected | ₦75k–₦150k |

If 50 contacts produce zero replies, the message is wrong — change the script,
not the volume. If demos happen but nobody buys, the offer or price is wrong.

## 9. SEO — what is done and what is next

Done (live):

- Unique title, description, keywords, canonical URL.
- Open Graph and Twitter cards with a generated 1200×630 image.
- `robots.txt` allowing the public site and blocking `/app`, `/platform`, auth
  and status pages.
- `sitemap.xml` submitted to search engines.
- Organization and WebSite structured data (JSON-LD).
- `noindex` on all private routes.
- Fast static landing page on Vercel's edge network.

Next 90 days:

1. Custom domain, then update `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` and Supabase redirect URLs.
2. Google Search Console + Bing Webmaster; verify and submit the sitemap.
3. Publish 8–12 useful articles targeting real searches, for example:
   - "how to manage staff across multiple shop branches in Nigeria"
   - "staff access control checklist for small retailers"
   - "Excel vs business management software for multi-branch shops"
4. Add a public pricing page and a demo-request form (WhatsApp link is enough
   to start; a form is better once traffic exists).
5. Earn links: GitHub profile, LinkedIn, business directories, association
   websites, guest posts.
6. Track impressions and clicks in Search Console monthly — not daily.

## 10. Decisions needed from the owner

1. Public contact: WhatsApp number and/or business email to put on the site.
2. Domain choice and purchase.
3. Approval of the pilot pricing above.
4. Whether to correct the landing-page claims now (recommended) or build the
   missing modules first.
5. Three to five named businesses for warm introductions.

Without items 1–4, any advertising spend is wasted: there is no way for an
interested visitor to become a customer.
